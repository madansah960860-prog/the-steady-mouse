Why “steady” is the whole idea

The name is a small promise. When something on the computer goes wrong, the natural response is a flutter of worry — and worry is exactly what makes us click the thing we should not, or hand over what we should keep. A steady hand is the real fix. Nearly every common trouble has a calm first step that solves it: switch it off and on, check the connection, read the label, pause before you act.

So that is how everything here is written. We never assume you have done something foolish, because you almost certainly have not. We simply show you the sensible next step, explain why it works, and leave you a little more confident than before. Over time, the troubles that once ruined an afternoon become five-minute fixes you handle without a second thought.

How The Steady Mouse helps

Most computer problems are not really broken machines — they are small misunderstandings between you and the device, or a connection that quietly slipped. Once you know the calm first thing to try, the trouble usually melts away. That is what we teach: the steady, sensible steps that fix the common problems, and the confidence to know you are not doing anything wrong.

We write for people who would rather understand their computer than be rescued by it. So instead of taking over, we explain. Instead of hurrying, we go one step at a time. And instead of selling you anything, we simply point you to the free, official way to get something done.

You will notice a few things we do on every page. We define each technical word the first time it appears, so you are never left behind by jargon. We tell you what you should see on the screen, so you know you are in the right place. And we always give the gentlest fix first, because the gentlest fix is usually the one that works. It is help written at the kitchen-table pace, by people who remember what it is like not to know.

An honest word about what we are

The Steady Mouse is an independent educational website, free to read from top to bottom. We do not sell products, we offer no repairs or paid help of any kind, and we have no way to reach into your computer. When we name a company — Microsoft, a printer brand, your internet provider — it is only to explain; we are not connected to any of them.

Please take a moment with the amber reminder at the top of every page. A genuine help website is for reading only. We will never telephone you, never ask to control your screen, and never ask for money. Anyone who does is pretending to be us, and the kindest thing you can do is hang up.

When your equipment needs something only its maker can handle — a warranty claim, a repair, recovering an account — type that company's official web address yourself and start there. We will always point you in that direction rather than stand between you and the people who made your device.

Who this is for

The Steady Mouse is for the capable adult who simply never had reason to learn the computer's quirks, and would rather understand them than be rescued. Perhaps you keep a notebook of passwords and a healthy suspicion of strange calls — good instincts both — and just want someone to explain the rest without rushing or talking down. That is precisely who we write for, and you are very welcome here. There is no test and no hurry; read what you need, when you need it, and come back as often as you like.

Where to begin

If something is misbehaving right now, go straight to the matching guide above. If you are simply building confidence, start with Home Wi-Fi — nearly everything else depends on a steady connection — and then read Scam Awareness, which protects everything you do online.

Safety reminder

No honest company phones out of the blue to ask for payment, passwords, or access to your computer. If a call or pop-up does, it is a scam — hang up or close it.

Report scams at reportfraud.ftc.gov.
