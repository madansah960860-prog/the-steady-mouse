Accessibility Statement

By the The Steady Mouse Editorial Team · Last reviewed: October 2026

Effective date: October 6, 2026. This accessibility statement applies to the The Steady Mouse website.

The Steady Mouse is built for comfortable reading, with older readers especially in mind. We aim to meet the W3C Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.1 at Level AA, and we go further where we can.

What we provide

  • Large body text with generous line spacing and a comfortable reading width.
  • A text-size control (A / A+ / A++) that remembers your choice.
  • Strong color contrast, underlined links, and a clearly visible keyboard focus outline.
  • Large tap targets and a mobile menu with a visible "Menu" label.
  • A print-friendly stylesheet for every long guide.
  • Meaningful alt text on images and a logical heading structure on every page.

Every interactive feature on The Steady Mouse can be operated with a keyboard, and the site remains fully readable with JavaScript turned off.

We also keep the writing itself as clear as the design. Sentences are short, paragraphs are brief, technical terms are explained the first time they appear, and procedures are broken into numbered steps. Good accessibility is not only about contrast and code; it is about being genuinely easy to read and to understand, and we work at that on The Steady Mouse as hard as we work at the rest.

The standard we aim for

At The Steady Mouse, we measure ourselves against the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines — the internationally recognized benchmark for making websites usable by people with a wide range of abilities, including those who use screen readers, who navigate by keyboard, or who need larger text and stronger contrast. The Steady Mouse aims for Level AA, the level most widely expected of public websites, and chooses the stricter Level AAA option wherever it serves our readers — for example, with extra-large body text and very high contrast.

Known limitations

We are honest about the gaps on The Steady Mouse. Some older guides may still be catching up to our latest spacing and contrast standards, and a few complex tables are best read on a wider screen even though they scroll sideways on a phone. Each one is corrected as it comes to our attention, and your reports speed that up.

Ongoing work

Accessibility is never truly finished. As The Steady Mouse grows and as the guidelines themselves evolve, we review pages, test with a keyboard and with text enlarged, and make improvements. If you rely on a particular assistive tool and something does not work as it should, telling us about it is the most useful thing you can do.

Report a barrier

If anything on The Steady Mouse is hard to read or use, please tell us at notes@thesteadymouse.com. Describe the page and what happened, and we will work to fix it and to help you reach the content in the meantime.

Contact us

Accessibility feedback is always welcome at notes@thesteadymouse.com.
