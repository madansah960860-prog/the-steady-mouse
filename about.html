About The Steady Mouse

By the The Steady Mouse Editorial Team · Last reviewed: October 2026

The Steady Mouse began with a simple observation: most “computer problems” are small, common, and far less frightening once someone explains them calmly. A printer that says “offline,” a Wi-Fi signal that fades, an email that looks a little off — these trip up capable people every day, not because the people lack sense, but because no one ever walked them through it without rushing.

Our calm approach

We write the way a patient neighbor would help: one step at a time, in plain words, telling you what you should see on the screen and what to do if you see something else. We favor the gentlest fix first — a restart, a cable check, a careful read of a label — because the gentlest fix usually works. And we always explain the “why,” so the next little trouble is one you can meet with confidence.

How our guides are made

Each guide is drafted, checked against how Windows and common equipment actually behave, and read by a second pair of eyes before it is published. Drafts may be prepared with AI assistance and are reviewed and edited by our editorial team before publishing, and every page carries a “Last reviewed” date so you can judge how current it is. When we are not sure of a fact, we leave it out rather than guess.

What we never do

The Steady Mouse is an independent educational publication for home computer users. We take that independence seriously, so here are our firm limits. We sell nothing. We offer no repairs and no paid help. We will never telephone you, never ask to control your computer, and never ask for payment. When your equipment needs something only its maker can do, we point you to that company's own official website and show you how to get there safely.

When we name a company, it is only to explain. The Steady Mouse is an independent educational website and is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or sponsored by any company mentioned.

About the photographs

The photographs here are openly licensed images from public collections, downloaded and stored on our own site rather than linked from elsewhere. Where a license requests credit, we name the photographer beneath the picture, and a full list lives in the site's image credits file.

Say hello

We would love to know which guide helped and what still puzzles you. Write any time to notes@thesteadymouse.com, and see our Contact page for what to expect.

Safety reminder

No honest company phones out of the blue to ask for payment, passwords, or access to your computer. If a call or pop-up does, it is a scam — hang up or close it.

Report scams at reportfraud.ftc.gov.
